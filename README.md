## The problem

India's National Air Quality Index is accurate and authoritative, but it has two
structural properties that limit its usefulness as a *personal* health signal:

1. **It is one number for everyone.** The index is the maximum of the pollutant
   sub-indices and is reported identically to every person in a city. A child
   with developing airways, someone with COPD, a cardiac patient and a healthy
   adult all receive the same figure and the same instruction, although the
   epidemiological literature documents materially different exposure-response
   relationships for each group.
2. **It is retrospective.** It describes air that has already been breathed. A
   parent deciding about tomorrow's outdoor sport needs an expectation, not a
   summary.

**AirGuard India forecasts the next day and resolves the advisory by cohort,
while remaining provably never less protective than the official index.**

## What is novel here

| | Contribution | Evidence it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ★ | **Health Advisory Index (HAI)** — a vulnerability-weighted composite for seven cohorts, grounded in cited exposure-response literature | At least one cohort receives a *different advisory band* from the one the official AQI implies on a large share of city-days. Decomposed into the aggregation effect and the cohort effect so the source of the divergence is visible. |
| ★ | **Never-less-protective guarantee** | Proved by assertion at every pipeline run, alongside boundedness, monotonicity in each pollutant and preservation of cohort ordering. |
| ★ | **Next-day forecasting, not nowcasting** | Strict time-ordered split; per-city lag and rolling features; scored against a persistence baseline so the learned model's actual contribution is visible. |
| ★ | **Leakage demonstrated, not just avoided** | Three classifiers on identical splits show that same-day band prediction is arithmetic dressed as machine learning — and that *no feature filter rescues it*, because the concentrations themselves determine the label. |
| ★ | **Imputation chosen by measurement** | Five strategies compared by a masking experiment; the best-scoring one is rejected for being non-causal, and the cost of that constraint is reported as a number. |
| ★ | **Event-aware cleaning** | The outlier rule exempts documented Diwali, crop-burning and lockdown windows, so genuine pollution episodes are never removed as outliers. |
| ★ | **Safe-exposure window recommender** | Built from instantaneous hourly concentrations, because the supplied hourly AQI is a 24-hour rolling quantity and is nearly flat within a day *by construction*. Both swings are measured and reported. |
| ★ | **City pollution archetypes** | Unsupervised clustering on the *composition* of the pollutant mix, mapped to distinct policy levers, and validated by reproducing the partition with a second algorithm. |
| ★ | **Counterfactual policy simulator** | Re-scores the whole record from first principles and reports *cohort advisory-days averted*. Sanity-checked against the measured effect of the 2020 lockdown. |
| ★ | **Explainability + Responsible AI** | TreeSHAP global and local explanations; data card and model card generated from results, with every limitation stated plainly. |

**Policy simulator**

Headline scenario — *PM2.5 and PM10 reduced by 20%*: mean AQI 144.1 → 120.3 (-16.5%), **29.8%** fewer unsafe city-days, and **12,432 cohort advisory-days averted**.

> **Independent sanity check.** The 2020 national lockdown produced a *measured* **-33.99%** change in national mean AQI. The simulator's nearest comparable scenario — a 40% reduction across all pollutants — yields **-37.0%**. The two agree closely, and the simulator was not fitted to that episode in any way.

## How leakage is handled

This is a time-series forecasting problem, and most of the ways to get it wrong
are subtle. Each hazard has a specific, stated control:

| Hazard | Control |
| --- | --- |
| **Temporal leakage** | Fixed calendar split; `shuffle` is never used; `TimeSeriesSplit` for all CV; an assertion fails the run if train and test dates overlap |
| **Cross-city leakage** | Every lag and rolling window computed with `groupby("City")`, so no city contributes to another's history |
| **Target leakage** | `AQI_next` and `AQI_Bucket_next` mutually excluded from each other's feature matrix; an assertion fails the run if either appears among the candidates |
| **Scaler / encoder leakage** | Scaling inside a `Pipeline`; city target encoding fitted on training rows only |
| **Imputation leakage** | All imputation statistics estimated on pre-boundary rows; the adopted strategy uses no forward-looking information within a city's series |
| **Resampling leakage** | SMOTE applied inside an `imblearn.Pipeline`, so it resamples within each training fold only |

**Same-day AQI is a legitimate predictor here**, because the target is
*tomorrow's* band — it is information genuinely available at prediction time.
The distinct leakage that a same-day nowcast suffers from is *measured* in
`results/evaluation_classification.json` under `leakage_demonstration`, and the
finding is sharper than expected: removing AQI, the sub-indices and every HAI
column still leaves the same-day task near-perfect, because the pollutant
concentrations themselves determine the label exactly. Same-day band prediction
is not a learning problem at all.

## Limitations, stated plainly

The full list is in [`reports/model_card.md`](reports/model_card.md). The four
that most constrain what this system can claim:

1. **No meteorology.** Wind, boundary-layer height, temperature and rainfall
   drive dispersion and are absent from the source data. This is the single
   largest constraint on achievable accuracy.
2. **The Health Advisory Index is not clinically validated.** The dataset
   contains no health outcomes, so no such validation was possible. The
   vulnerability factors encode the *direction and relative ordering* of
   documented associations; they are not a meta-analytic pooling of effect
   sizes, and no published risk ratio is reproduced. **It is an
   advisory-prioritisation instrument, not a clinical risk score.**
3. **Twelve cities, urban only, record ends 1 July 2020.** Nothing here
   describes present-day air quality or rural exposure.
4. **The model under-predicts the most severe days.** Inherent to minimising an
   average loss on a right-skewed target; stated rather than concealed, and the
   reason the app shows the predicted band with its confidence rather than the
   point estimate alone.

> **This system provides general air quality guidance for public-health
> awareness. It is not medical advice and must not replace consultation with a
> qualified clinician. The official CPCB Air Quality Index remains the
> authoritative index for India.**
