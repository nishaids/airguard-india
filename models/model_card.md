## 1. Overview

AirGuard India comprises three models and one deterministic index.

| Component | Model | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regression | LightGBM | Forecast tomorrow's AQI value |
| Classification | XGBoost + SMOTE | Forecast tomorrow's CPCB band |
| Clustering | K-Means, k = 2 | Discover city pollution archetypes |
| Health Advisory Index | Deterministic formula | Cohort-resolved advisory, no fitting involved |

## 2. Intended use

**Intended.** Informational next-day air quality guidance for the general public and for vulnerable cohorts; teaching and research on vulnerability-weighted indexing; exploratory policy-target comparison through the counterfactual simulator.

**Intended users.** Residents of the twelve covered cities, public-health communicators, urban local bodies, schools and hospitals planning outdoor activity, and students of data science.

## 3. Out-of-scope use

* **Not medical advice.** The Health Advisory Index prioritises advisory messages; it is not a clinical risk score and must not be used to make or defer a treatment decision. No component of this system has been validated against health outcomes.
* **Not a regulatory instrument.** The official CPCB AQI remains the authoritative index. Nothing here should be used for compliance assessment, enforcement, or any legal purpose.
* **Not a real-time system.** The models are fitted on a record ending 1 July 2020 and are served here over historical data. They are not connected to a live feed.
* **Not valid outside the twelve retained cities**, and not valid for rural areas at all.
* **Not a policy effectiveness model.** The simulator states what the advisory burden would be at a given concentration. It does not predict whether any intervention would achieve that concentration.

## 4. Training data and split

* Training period: 2015-01-01 to 2019-05-25 (13,559 rows).
* Test period: 2019-05-26 to 2020-07-01 (4,836 rows).
* The split is a fixed calendar boundary at `2019-05-26`; the training period strictly precedes the test period and shuffling is never used.
* Cross-validation: `TimeSeriesSplit` with 4 expanding-window folds.
* The row counts above are for the cleaned daily table. The supervised matrices are smaller: 10,331 training and 4,397 test rows, because a row is usable only once its 14-day rolling window is complete and only if it carries a labelled next-day target. Those rows are dropped rather than back-filled, since a window that never existed cannot honestly be reconstructed.

## 6. The Health Advisory Index

### 6.1 Construction

For a city-day, each CPCB pollutant sub-index is rescaled to 0-100, multiplied by a cohort vulnerability factor, and combined as the dominant cohort-adjusted sub-index plus 0.25 times the mean of the remainder, capped at 100.

### 6.2 Properties, proved by assertion in `src/features/hai.py`

* Bounded on [0, 100].
* **Never less protective than the official AQI**, for every cohort.
* Monotonically non-decreasing in every pollutant concentration.
* Cohort ordering preserved: no cohort ever receives a lower index than the healthy-adult reference.
* CPCB breakpoints reproduced exactly at every published anchor.

### 6.4 Status of these factors, stated plainly

**The vulnerability factors are a constructed research prototype.** They encode the direction and the relative ordering of associations documented in the sources listed in the docstring of `src/features/hai.py` (WHO 2021; the US EPA Integrated Science Assessments for particulate matter, ozone, oxides of nitrogen, sulfur oxides and carbon monoxide; Pope and Dockery 2006; Brook et al. 2010; Gauderman et al. 2015; Orellano et al. 2017; Pedersen et al. 2013). They are **not** the output of a formal meta-analytic pooling of effect sizes, and **no claim is made that they reproduce any published risk ratio**. The index is an advisory-prioritisation instrument, not a clinical risk score.

**The index has not been validated against health outcomes.** The dataset contains no hospital admissions, no mortality and no symptom data, so no such validation was possible. What has been demonstrated is internal consistency (the properties in section 6.2) and divergence from the official AQI on real observations, which establishes that the index carries information the AQI does not, without establishing that the information is clinically calibrated.

## 7. Explainability

TreeSHAP exact Shapley values are computed for both deployed models. Shapley attribution is used because it is the only additive attribution satisfying efficiency: contributions for a single prediction sum exactly to the gap between that prediction and the model's base value, so a local explanation is complete rather than indicative.

Most influential features for the regressor: `HAI_healthy_adult`, `PM2.5`, `AQI`, `AQI_roll7_mean`, `total_pollutant_load`, `City_target_enc`, `PM2.5_delta1`, `AQI_delta1`.

## 8. Limitations

1. **No meteorological features.** Wind, boundary-layer height, temperature and rainfall drive dispersion and are absent from the source data. This is the dominant constraint on achievable accuracy.
2. **Twelve cities only.** Fourteen of twenty-six cities failed the completeness or volume gates and were excluded, each with a stated reason. The models are not valid elsewhere, and are not valid for rural areas.
3. **Station coverage bias.** City-level means represent monitored locations, not population-weighted exposure, and monitoring coverage expanded over the record, so early and late years describe different city sets.
4. **Imputation.** 10.783% of pollutant cells are imputed. The strategy was selected by a masking experiment and is causal, but imputed values are estimates and carry their own error into every downstream figure.
5. **The record ends 1 July 2020.** Nothing here describes present-day air quality, and the most recent period in the data is atypical because of the lockdown.
6. **Under-prediction of extreme days.** Residual bias turns negative in the upper bands: the model under-predicts the most severe days. This is inherent to minimising an average loss on a right-skewed target and is the reason the application presents the predicted band with its confidence rather than the point estimate alone.
7. **Clustering is low-powered.** Twelve cities is a small sample; the archetypes are an interpretive organising device for policy discussion, not a claim of sharply bounded natural classes.
8. **No atmospheric chemistry in the simulator.** Pollutants are reduced independently, so ozone-led scenarios in particular should be read with caution, since reducing nitrogen oxides can raise local ozone under some urban conditions.
9. **The Health Advisory Index is unvalidated against outcomes** (section 6.4).

## 9. Ethical considerations and disclaimer

No personal data is used at any stage. The principal risk is an advisory that understates exposure, which is mitigated by the construction guarantee that the Health Advisory Index is never less protective than the official AQI, and by reporting the severity-understatement rate explicitly.

> **This system provides general air quality guidance for public-health awareness. It is not medical advice and must not replace consultation with a qualified clinician. The official CPCB Air Quality Index remains the authoritative index for India.**
