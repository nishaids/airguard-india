# AI disclosure audit

## Policy checked

IEEE Conferences author-ethics submission policies, "Guidelines for Artificial Intelligence (AI)-Generated Text", fetched 13 October 2026 from
<https://conferences.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/author-ethics/guidelines-and-policies/submission-policies/>.

Policy text, quoted:

- The use of content generated by artificial intelligence (AI) in an article "shall be disclosed in the acknowledgments section of any article submitted to an IEEE publication."
- "The AI system used shall be identified" and the affected sections "shall be identified and accompanied by a brief explanation regarding the level at which the AI system was used to generate the content."
- Exception: "The use of AI systems for editing and grammar enhancement is common practice" and "is generally outside the intent of the above policy", for which "disclosure as noted above is not required, but recommended."

## Determination

The editing-and-grammar exception does not apply: AI systems drafted manuscript text and wrote analysis and figure code, which is generated content. The disclosure is therefore **mandatory and must stay inside the manuscript, in the acknowledgments**. It was not removed.

Shortest accurate wording retained in `main.tex`:

> Generative AI systems (OpenAI ChatGPT and Codex, and Anthropic Claude) were used to draft text and analysis and figure code. The author verified all numerical statements and references.

## If the target conference differs

If the chosen venue publishes its own policy that supersedes the IEEE wording, use that venue's required form. Removing the disclosure entirely is only defensible if the venue does not require in-paper disclosure of AI-generated content; no such venue has been selected or verified, so the statement stays.

No detector evasion was attempted, and no mandatory disclosure was concealed.
