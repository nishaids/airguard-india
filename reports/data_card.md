**Threshold justification.** Two independent gates are applied. The completeness gate (80% of the calendar days in a city's own observation span must carry a labelled AQI) ensures that per-city lag and rolling features are computed over a near-contiguous series rather than across long gaps, since a lag of one row is only a lag of one day when the series is dense. The volume gate (at least 730 labelled days, i.e. two years) ensures each retained city contributes at least two full annual cycles, so that seasonal structure is estimable and a time-ordered split leaves a usable test period. Both thresholds were fixed before any model was fitted and are not tuned against model performance.

## 5. Collection and representativeness

Measurements come from the Continuous Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Station network operated by CPCB. Three biases follow from how that network is built and should condition any reading of these results.

* **Station siting bias.** Monitors are concentrated in larger cities and within those cities are not sited at random. A city-level daily mean represents the locations that happen to be monitored, not the population-weighted exposure of everyone living there.
* **Coverage expanded over time.** Most cities enter the record part-way through, so the early years describe a smaller and systematically different set of cities than the later years. Any apparent national trend across the full span is confounded with this change in composition, and no such trend is claimed in this project.
* **Urban only.** The dataset contains no rural monitoring, although a large share of the Indian population and a large share of biomass-burning exposure is rural.

## 6. What the dataset does not contain

* **No meteorology.** Wind speed, boundary-layer height, temperature, humidity and rainfall drive dispersion and are absent. This is the single largest omission in the feature set, and it bounds achievable forecast accuracy. Season and calendar position act only as weak proxies.
* **No emission inventories.** Traffic volume, industrial output, construction activity and crop-burning fire counts are absent, so source attribution in this project is inferred from pollutant ratios rather than measured.
* **No health outcomes.** There are no hospital admissions, no mortality and no symptom data. The Health Advisory Index is therefore validated for internal consistency and for divergence from the AQI, and **not** against observed health outcomes. This is stated again in the model card.
* **No sub-city resolution.** A single value per city per day cannot represent the difference between a roadside and a residential location.
* **Record ends 1 July 2020.** The dataset does not cover later years, so nothing here describes present-day air quality.

## 7. Limitations

These limitations bound what any analysis built on this dataset can claim. They are properties of the data, not of the methods applied to it.

1. **No meteorological variables.** Wind speed, boundary-layer height, temperature, humidity and rainfall drive dispersion and are absent entirely. This is the single largest omission and it bounds achievable forecast accuracy for any model fitted on this record.
2. **No health outcome data.** There are no admissions, no mortality and no symptom records, so no index derived from this dataset can be validated against the outcomes it is meant to predict.
3. **Station siting bias.** A city-level daily mean represents the locations that happen to be monitored, not the population-weighted exposure of residents.
4. **Compositional change over time.** Monitoring coverage expanded across the record, so the set of cities represented in 2015 differs from the set represented in 2020. Any apparent national trend across the full span is confounded with this change.
5. **Urban only.** No rural monitoring, although a large share of the Indian population and of biomass-burning exposure is rural.
6. **No sub-city resolution.** One value per city per day cannot distinguish a roadside from a residential location.
7. **Record ends 1 July 2020.** Nothing derived from it describes present-day air quality, and its final months are atypical because of the national lockdown.
8. **Imputed values are estimates.** The strategy was selected by measurement and is causal, but imputed cells carry their own error into every downstream figure.

> **Disclaimer.** Any advisory derived from this dataset is general public-health guidance. It is not medical advice and must not replace consultation with a qualified clinician. The official CPCB Air Quality Index remains the authoritative index for India.

## 8. Intended and out-of-scope use

**Intended.** Academic study of air quality forecasting and of vulnerability-weighted indexing; public-health communication research; demonstration of a cohort-resolved advisory system.

**Out of scope.** Regulatory compliance assessment; legal or enforcement use; clinical decision-making; any claim about present-day air quality; any claim about cities or rural areas outside the twelve retained.

## 9. Ethical considerations

The dataset contains no personal data and no individual-level records, so there is no privacy risk in its use. The principal ethical risk lies in the *output* rather than the input: an advisory that understates risk could lead a vulnerable person to expose themselves unnecessarily. That risk is addressed by constructing the Health Advisory Index so that it can never be less protective than the official AQI (proved by assertion in `src/features/hai.py`), by reporting the rate at which the classifier understates severity as an explicit safety statistic, and by carrying a medical disclaimer on every advisory the application issues.
